This tool is a deliberately simple, zero-dimensional energy-balance model. It is designed to show cause and effect, not to predict future climate. The controls change four quantities: methane concentration, carbon-dioxide concentration, cloud fraction and cloud albedo.

Surface temperature

Earth absorbs solar radiation and emits infrared radiation. If there were no infrared-absorbing atmosphere, the absorbed solar energy would set an effective temperature. Greenhouse gases increase the infrared optical depth of the model atmosphere, producing a surface temperature above the effective temperature.

Tsurface = Teff (1 + 0.75 τ)1/4

Methane, CH4

Methane absorbs infrared radiation. The simulator converts the methane concentration in ppm to a partial pressure and uses a simple square-root approximation for its contribution to infrared optical depth.

τCH4 = 0.725 √eCH4

The coefficient is intentionally simplified for teaching. The visual cows are a reminder that ruminants are one source of atmospheric methane; they are not a quantitative emissions model.

Carbon dioxide, CO2

Carbon dioxide also contributes to infrared optical depth. The model uses:

τCO2 = 0.029 √eCO2

Water vapour is included as a fixed background contribution rather than as an interactive feedback:

τH2O = 0.087 √eH2O
τ = τCO2 + τCH4 + τH2O

Cloud fraction

Cloud fraction is the fraction of the model planet covered by cloud. In this simulator, cloud affects temperature through reflected sunlight only. Real clouds also interact strongly with infrared radiation; that effect is deliberately omitted here.

Cloud albedo

Albedo is the fraction of incoming sunlight that is reflected. The simulator combines cloud albedo, Ac, with a fixed clear-sky surface albedo, As = 0.15, to estimate planetary albedo:

Ap = cf Ac + (1 − cf) As

The effective temperature then follows from the global solar-energy balance:

Teff = [ S (1 − Ap) / (4 σ) ]1/4

Model limitations

Use the simulator to investigate tendencies, not realistic climate states.
  • The atmosphere is represented by one global average rather than latitude, altitude, seasons or weather.
  • Water vapour is fixed, so the important water-vapour feedback is absent.
  • Clouds affect short-wave reflection only; their infrared greenhouse effect is omitted.
  • The optical-depth relationships are simple teaching approximations.
  • The model has no ocean heat storage, ice-albedo feedback, carbon cycle or atmospheric dynamics.
  • Extreme values such as 100% cloud with 100% cloud albedo are mathematical experiments, not plausible Earth states.

The reference sliders reproduce the settings used by the original teaching simulator. They are model reference values rather than a claim about present-day atmospheric composition.
